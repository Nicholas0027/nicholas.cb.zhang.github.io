Stencil ACL, the lab's mark

Three block letters cut from one stencil. The A splits at the apex into a human leg and an agent leg, and the two touch only through the white bar: the point where people and AI agents meet. The C and L are cut the same way, so the three letters read as one family. It is now the mark of both sites.

Construction

One height, one stroke, one cut

Every letter is 40 units tall with 10-unit strokes, and every stencil cut is vertical and 3 units wide.

People and agentsOnly the A carries colour: the amber leg is people, the blue leg is AI agents. They meet at the white bar and nowhere else.
Parallel legsBoth legs keep one width from foot to apex, so the A holds the same weight as the C and L.
One stencilThe A is cut at its apex, the C where the curve meets the arms, and the L where the stem meets the foot.
City lab in whiteThe C and L stay white on dark and black on light, so the human–agent pair always leads.
Clear spaceKeep one stroke (10 units) free on every side. Never outline, stretch or recolour the legs.
Smallest sizeThe full mark down to 16 px tall; the A alone below that, for favicons and app icons.
What changed

From version 3 to version 4

Version 3
Version 4
  • The legs of the A have parallel edges; in version 3 they tapered towards the apex.
  • The white bar sits inside the A and no longer cuts across the coloured legs.
  • The C is round, so it reads as a C, not an E or a bracket, even at 16 px.
  • All three letters share one cap height, one stroke and one 3-unit cut.
  • Letter spacing is set by eye: tighter after the A, a little wider after the open C.
  • The A on its own is the new favicon and avatar.
Colour

People, agents, city

Amber always means people and blue always means AI agents, in the logo, the glyphs and the live simulation. White marks where they interact. Figures from the papers keep their own colours on the dark ground.

Glyph system

One grid, many meetings

Each research stage and project can take its own glyph: a human path, an agent path and the node where they meet, drawn on the same five-by-five grid of intersections.
